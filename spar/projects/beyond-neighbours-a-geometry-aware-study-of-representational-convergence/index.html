Beyond Neighbours: A Geometry Aware Study of Representational Convergence

  • Trinidad BorrellParis Brain Institute · Forschungszentrum Jülich

Do models share a transferable concept geometry, or only local neighbour structure? This project re-tests cross-model representational convergence with curvature-aware distances instead of relying only on Euclidean comparisons.

About the project

Recent calibrated evaluations challenge claims of global representational convergence while preserving evidence for local neighbourhood alignment. But the standard global metrics assume a flat ambient space, even when concept representations may lie on curved manifolds.

The team will reproduce the calibrated baseline and compare it with geodesic, diffusion, and Gromov–Wasserstein methods. A positive result supports model-agnostic manifold interventions; a negative result argues for validating control tools separately on every model.

Theory of change

Oversight tools often assume probes or steering methods transfer because models share internal structure. Measuring the kind of geometry those methods actually use can either justify that transfer or expose it as an unsafe assumption.

Your role

Mentees act as co-investigators, owning a research thread from literature and implementation through interpretation and writing.

Prerequisites

  • Comfort with linear algebra and basic manifold geometry.
  • Working knowledge of representation-similarity metrics.
  • Solid Python and PyTorch experience with existing research codebases.

Application questions

  • Explain how metric assumptions could hide global convergence after calibration, then propose a concrete first experiment under a $1,000 compute budget.
