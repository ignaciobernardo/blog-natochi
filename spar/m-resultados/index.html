Why apply?

  • Research support

    Gain access to qualified applicants to advance your research proposals. Who you choose to work with is entirely up to you, and there's no commitment to take on any fellows if none are a good fit.

  • Funding up to $30k

    SPAR projects can apply for grants of up to $30k for research expenses like compute and API access.

  • Long-term collaborators

    Many SPAR mentors end up inviting their fellows back to work on additional projects after the program ends, or hiring them to their teams.

  • Build your field

    Help train the next generation of researchers to do crucial work like yours.

What it takes

  • 2–10

    hours a week supervising your fellows, at the level of involvement you choose.

  • 3

    months, with options for extension, culminating in a Demo Day attended by top AI safety orgs.

  • 62

    applications for the median project in Fall 2025. The top 20% received over 100.

Submit a research project proposal in one of SPAR's focus areas: AI safety, AI policy, AI security, interpretability, biosecurity or societal impacts. Fellows apply to your project, and you make all the decisions, from what to ask in the application to which fellows to accept. You can even choose not to move forward with anyone.

The SPAR team promotes your project, handles administrative work and, if needed, funds compute, software and other expenses. Fellows make steady progress through regular written reports, and many mentor-fellow pairs keep working together after the three months.

Express interest as a mentor

Am I eligible?

We typically accept researchers with AI safety or biosecurity research experience matching or exceeding that of a late PhD student or a MATS scholar, in technical AI safety, policy and governance, or biosecurity research.

Senior graduate students, postdocs, academic faculty and professionals at a lab can suggest any project within SPAR's focus areas. Junior graduate students and MATS scholars should propose projects adjacent to their existing research focus.

If you don't fit any of these categories, it must be very clear that you have legible credentials to lead the project you propose. We accept undergraduate applicants to mentor only in exceptional circumstances.

FAQ

Is my project a good fit?

Your project may be a good fit if:

  • It can be scoped for 3 months of work at 5-20 hr/week/fellow, at a team size you're comfortable supervising
  • It has a strong theory of change for tackling large-scale risks from advanced AI, with an emphasis on existential risks
  • It's focused on AI technical safety, AI policy and governance, AI strategy, AI security, or biosecurity

For biosecurity projects, we don't require projects to be focused on AI risks specifically, but we do require their theories of change to potentially be helpful in preventing or mitigating AI-enabled bioattacks (exclusively or in addition to other threats). Given the remote nature of the program, we typically won't accept projects involving laboratory work.

See here for examples of projects SPAR has accepted in the past.

How qualified are typical SPAR applicants?

In our Fall 2025 cohort (which did not include biosecurity projects), about half of accepted applicants had worked a job or published research in ML. Some typical profiles of accepted AI safety applicants include:

  • Graduate student with strong publication record and 2-4 years research experience in ML or in technology policy
  • Top-tier undergraduate with research internships at major companies or labs
  • Mid-career professional with 5-10 years in software or data science, transitioning to AI safety

We expect applicants to have some relevant technical background to the projects at hand, but not all projects will receive applicants with deep research experience.

What if I don't get any applicants I'm satisfied with?

You can just withdraw from the program. Submitting a SPAR project doesn't create any commitment to move forward unless you find fellows you're excited about. However, we find withdrawals are rare.

How will fellows be assigned to my project?

Fellows will apply to your project and answer any custom application questions you've included. You'll review applications, including scheduling any interviews or trial tasks you'd like. Please note you may need to set aside substantial time for application review—in Fall 2025, the median project received 62 applications, while the top 20% received over 100.

After reviewing applications, you'll send us a ranked shortlist of candidates, and applicants will also submit a preference ranking among projects they've applied to. We'll run an algorithm that matches fellows with projects based on both mentor and fellow preferences, as well as other factors like ensuring no one gets assigned more hours of work than they're available for. While this means you're not guaranteed to be matched with any given applicant, we will never assign you fellows who weren't on your shortlist.

What does SPAR expect of mentors?
  • Review fellow applications by SPAR's deadlines, including arranging any interviews or assessments you're interested in. (The SPAR team will provide resources and guidance for managing this process.)
  • Coordinate weekly meetings with your project team.
  • Have weekly or biweekly 1:1s with each of your fellows. (There's also an option to give high-level asynchronous feedback only, though our bar for accepting these projects is higher.)
  • Be reasonably accessible to your fellows over a platform like Slack, Discord, Signal, Whatsapp, etc.
  • Set up a task management system to coordinate your fellows' to-dos.
  • Anything else that's needed to ensure your project stays on track.

The mentor application asks you to commit to a level of involvement per fellow, from 5hr/wk/fellow to high-level asynchronous feedback. Whatever commitment you make, we ask that you honor it.

How many fellows can my project take on?

SPAR projects can have as few as one fellow or as many as you'd like. If you accept a particularly large team, the SPAR team may check in to ensure you can commit appropriate time to supervising each fellow.

Can there be more than one mentor on a project?

Yes, mentors are welcome to team up on projects!

Can one mentor run multiple projects?

Yes! You can run as many SPAR projects as you'd like, as long as you can commit appropriate time to your fellows. You can also submit many project proposals and later decide which ones to go forward with, based on the received interest and quality of applications.
