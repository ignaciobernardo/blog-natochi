What is SPAR?

SPAR is a part-time, remote research program pairing aspiring researchers with professionals addressing risks from AI to work together on impactful research projects. Mentees gain research experience and guidance, and mentors get assistance with their projects.

Mentors can choose to commit anywhere from 2–10 hours/week.

The program runs for 3 months with options for extension, culminating in a Demo Day attended by top AI safety orgs.

We typically look for mentors with research experience matching that of a late PhD student or a MATS scholar. We're excited to receive applications in technical AI safety, policy, or governance, or biosecurity research.

What research does SPAR support?

SPAR supports a broad range of research that we think is helpful for helping society mitigate the risks posed by advanced AI, including but not limited to:

AI Safety — ensuring powerful AI systems follow human intentions.

AI Policy — governing the development of transformative AI.

AI Security — hardware-enabled governance, and preventing the theft of model weights.

Interpretability — reverse-engineering neural networks and their internal representations.

Biosecurity — preventing or preparing for AI-enabled bioattacks.

Societal impacts — understanding the effect of transformative AI on society and the economy.

Why apply?

Mentoring a SPAR project is a few hours a week, and you decide who you work with and whether you take anyone on at all.

Research support — gain access to qualified applicants to advance your research proposals. Who you choose to work with is entirely up to you, and there's no commitment to take on any mentees if none are a good fit.

Funding — SPAR projects can apply for grants of up to $30k for research expenses like compute and API access.

Find long-term collaborators — many SPAR mentors end up inviting their mentees back to work on additional projects after the program ends, or hiring them to their teams.

Build your field — help train the next generation of researchers to do crucial work like yours.

Am I eligible?

We typically accept applications from researchers with AI safety or biosecurity research experience matching or exceeding that of a late PhD student or a MATS scholar. We're excited to receive applications in technical AI safety, policy and governance, or biosecurity.

If you are a senior graduate student, postdoc, academic faculty member, or professional at a lab, you can suggest any project within SPAR’s focus areas. If you are a junior graduate student or a MATS scholar, your project should be adjacent to your existing research focus.

If you don't fit any of these categories, it must be very clear that you have legible credentials to lead the project you propose. We accept undergraduate applicants to mentor only in exceptional circumstances.

What's it like to be a SPAR mentor?

Submit a research project proposal in one of SPAR's focus areas. Mentees will apply to your project, and you can take on as many as you're excited about. You make all the decisions here, from what to ask in the application to which mentees to accept. You can even choose not to move forward with anyone if you don't find a good match.

The SPAR team will promote your project and handle administrative work. We’ll also provide guidance for managing the application process, and, if needed, provide you with funding for compute, software, and other expenses.

Once you've chosen your mentees, you'll spend 2–10 hours a week supervising their work as they move your project forward. SPAR provides resources to support your collaboration and ensures mentees make steady progress by requiring regular written reports.

While SPAR officially runs for three months, many mentor-mentee pairs choose to continue their work beyond this point and may collaborate on additional projects in the future.

FAQ

Is my project a good fit?

Your project may be a good fit if it can be scoped for 3 months of work at 5–20 hours per week, at a team size you are comfortable supervising; it has a clear theory of change for mitigating catastrophic risks from advanced AI; and it is focused on AI technical safety, AI policy and governance, AI strategy, AI security, or biosecurity.

How qualified are typical SPAR applicants?

Most applicants are graduate students or early-career professionals with a technical or policy background. Rounds regularly include postdocs and people moving across from adjacent fields.

What if I don't get any applicants I'm satisfied with?

You are never obliged to take anyone on. If nobody is a good match you can close your project without accepting a mentee, and we will help you rework the proposal for the next round if you want to try again.

How will mentees be assigned to my project?

They are not assigned. Mentees apply to the projects they want, you read the applications, and you decide who to shortlist and accept.

What does SPAR expect of mentors?

Two to ten hours a week for three months, a written project proposal up front, and enough contact with your mentees that they can make steady progress. We handle the promotion and the administration.

How many mentees can my project take on?

As many as you are confident you can supervise well. Most projects take one to three.

Can there be more than one mentor on a project?

Yes. Co-mentoring is common, and it is a good way to take on a larger team or to share the load across a busy quarter.

Can one mentor run multiple projects?

Yes, though we suggest keeping the total commitment inside the same 2–10 hours a week.
