When Do Models Learn Time? Tracing Temporal Representations Across Training Stages

Time
10 hrs/week
Team
4–10 mentees
Mentorship
Guidance (1+ hr/week/mentee)
  • Marc KaufmannIndependent
  • Justin ShenkIndependent AI Safety Researcher

At which stage of training do temporal representations emerge, and when can they still be controlled? This project traces temporal capability and internal representations across pretraining checkpoints, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning.

About the project

Earlier work localized a temporal-preference circuit and showed that a model’s time horizon has a nonlinear internal geometry. This follow-up asks when that structure first appears and how it changes through the training pipeline.

The team will compare open intermediate checkpoints, design tasks where temporal reasoning is necessary, and test probing, patching, and steering at multiple stages. The intended output is a technical report and reusable temporal-probing code.

Theory of change

Knowing when temporal preference becomes load-bearing tells us which safety interventions remain available. A representation fixed in pretraining calls for monitoring and steering; one introduced later could be shaped through data and training design.

Your role

Mentees will run experiments semi-independently, analyze results, and contribute to the write-up, with weekly direction and responsive mentor support.

Prerequisites

  • Proficiency in Python and PyTorch.
  • Comfort extracting transformer activations and working with linear probes.
  • At least one hands-on project involving transformer models.

Application questions

  1. Give two explanations for a sudden jump in temporal-probe accuracy across checkpoints and one experiment that distinguishes them.
  2. Identify a confound that could let a model solve a temporal task without representing time horizons, and propose a control.
