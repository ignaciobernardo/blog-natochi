  • This code applies to everyone taking part in M3: fellows, facilitators, guest speakers, mentors in office hours, and the M3 team. It covers every M3 space, in person and online: week one, pod calls, standups, office hours, demo day, the fellows platform and any group chat set up for the cohort.

    It also applies when you represent M3 elsewhere, for example when you introduce yourself as an M3 fellow at a conference or in a message to a lab or funder.

  • M3 brings together senior people from very different backgrounds. That only works if everyone can disagree hard about ideas while staying generous with each other.

    • Critique the work, not the person. Red-teaming a project is the point of week one; mocking someone is not.
    • Assume good faith and ask before you conclude. People are often new to the field or to your field.
    • Make room. Notice who hasn't spoken, and keep your turns short in group sessions.
    • No harassment, intimidation, discrimination or unwanted physical contact, of any kind and toward anyone.
    • No sexual attention or advances in M3 spaces. If you are unsure whether something is welcome, it isn't.
    • Respect people's time: show up when you said you would, and say early when you can't.

  • Week one is the most intense part of the program and the one most people take leave from work for. Treat it that way.

    • Attend every core block (research mornings, afternoon sprints and talks) unless you have told the team in advance.
    • Follow the venue's rules, including its security, guest and quiet-area policies. Venues are hosted by partner organisations; their staff's instructions apply.
    • Bring guests into the venue only with the team's agreement.
    • Drink responsibly at social events. Being impaired is not an excuse for behaviour that breaks this code.
    • Look out for each other, especially travelling to and from the venue late.

  • Your pod is three or four fellows and a facilitator who meet weekly through the build phase. The pod is your accountability, so it only works if everyone shows up for it.

    • Post your async standup each week: what you did, what's next, where you're blocked.
    • Attend the weekly call, or tell your pod beforehand and post your update anyway.
    • Give honest, specific feedback, and take it without getting defensive.
    • If your project stalls or you need to step back, tell your facilitator early. Pausing is fine; disappearing is not.

  • Fellows, guests and mentors will share unpublished research, early ideas, details of their employers and sometimes sensitive security information.

    • Sessions are off the record by default. Don't quote or attribute what someone said without their permission.
    • Don't share anything marked or described as confidential outside the people it was shared with.
    • Never bring your employer's confidential information, source code or credentials into M3, and don't ask others to.
    • Ask before you photograph slides, whiteboards or screens.
    • Don't post other people's projects or ideas publicly as your own.

  • Some M3 projects touch real attack surfaces: model weights, agent tooling, hardware, critical infrastructure. Security work in M3 must stay legal, authorised and careful.

    • Only test systems you own or have explicit written permission to test.
    • If you find a vulnerability in a real system, follow the owner's disclosure process, or ask the M3 team to help you reach them. Don't publish it before the owner has had a reasonable chance to fix it.
    • If your work could meaningfully increase someone's ability to cause serious harm (for example a new attack on model weights, or uplift for cyber or bio misuse), stop and talk to the team before sharing it, even within the cohort.
    • Write-ups, repos and demo-day talks may need redaction. The team will help you decide, not decide for you.

  • M3 pays for the hours you put into your project, at your rate up to $100 an hour. That depends on trust.

    • Log only hours you actually spent on your M3 project, and log them in the week you worked them.
    • Check that taking part is compatible with your employment contract and any visa conditions. Tell the team if anything changes.
    • Declare conflicts of interest: if you, your employer or a company you have a stake in could benefit from your project or from introductions M3 makes.
    • Don't use M3 introductions to sell to or recruit from the people you meet without their agreement.

    • Sessions are not recorded unless the speaker agrees and everyone in the room is told.
    • M3 takes photos and short interviews during week one. You can opt out of photos, of interviews, or of being named at any time; tell the team and it will be respected, including for material already taken.
    • M3 only publishes your name, photo, quotes or project with your consent.
    • When you talk about M3 publicly, speak for yourself, not for M3 or its partners.

  • If something happens that makes you or someone else uncomfortable or unsafe, or you think this code has been broken, tell us. You don't need to be sure, and you don't need to have been directly affected.

    • Talk to any member of the M3 team in person, or write to fernando@m3fellowship.com.
    • If the concern involves a member of the M3 team, you can raise it with one of the partner organisations instead.
    • We will listen, keep it as confidential as we can while acting on it, and tell you what we decided to do.
    • Nobody will face retaliation for raising a concern in good faith. Retaliation is itself a breach of this code.

    If anyone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first.

  • The team will respond in proportion to what happened. Depending on the situation, that can mean:

    • a conversation and a clear request to change behaviour;
    • being asked to leave a session or the venue;
    • removal from the program, including the end of paid hours from that point;
    • informing a partner organisation or venue where their rules or people are involved.

    Where possible, the team will hear from everyone involved before deciding. This code will be reviewed after each cohort; suggestions are welcome.
